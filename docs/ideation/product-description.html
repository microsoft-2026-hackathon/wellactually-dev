WHAT IT IS

A service that helps AI-native junior developers selectively pair with AI in their existing projects to apply engineering methods, design principles, and know-how established before coding agents to real work.

It does not generate educational projects for you. Thinking through the work you are already doing together provides the learning context.

01 / TARGET USER

AI-native junior developers

Our Primary Customer is a junior developer who started building with coding agents and has had few opportunities to apply established engineering methods, design principles, and practical know-how firsthand. This product is for people with little or no pair-programming experience who want to learn what to consider during development.

What they can do

Build and deploy

  • Ask coding agents to implement features.
  • Connect tools and services to build working products.
  • Use development environments and deployment workflows.
Experience they want to gain

Engineering judgment

  • Why choose an approach, and what are the alternatives?
  • Which assumptions, exceptions, and change effects need review?
  • What should be investigated first, and how much should be delegated?

Use it in projects already underway: a personal project, a learning project the user created, or a company product. Wellactually does not supply a separate educational project.

Not every junior developer experiences this problem. Our initial audience is people who feel this gap and want to fill it through real work. Experienced developers may also benefit in unfamiliar domains, but these juniors' experience sets the initial product standard.

02 / PROBLEM & EVIDENCE

A capability-formation gap

Delegating problem decomposition, design choices, and error interpretation to an agent can produce results while leaving fewer opportunities to apply the engineering methods and design know-how involved. The central problem is not the decay of existing skills, but a lack of experience learning what to think through and how to evaluate it.

Observable outcomes

Implementation and passing tests

We can verify that a feature was built. But why a cache is needed and how stale its values may be are separate judgments.

≠
Capability to develop

Design judgment that fits the constraints

Developers need to distinguish bottlenecks from freshness requirements, explain the costs of alternatives, and apply that reasoning independently to the next similar problem.

What primary studies show—and what their findings alone cannot establish
Study Findings Limits of interpretation
Learning a new library
Shen & Tamkin, 2026 [R1] 		A randomized experiment with 52 participants experienced in Python. On an unaided assessment after a new Trio task, the AI-assisted group scored significantly lower on a quiz covering concepts, code comprehension, and error diagnosis. This was an immediate learning assessment. The sample was not limited to AI-native early-career developers, and the study did not measure long-term design or architecture capability.
Novices' working process
Prather et al., 2024 [R2] 		Twenty of 21 introductory students completed the program, but some struggled to judge whether AI suggestions were appropriate or what to do next. This was an observational study. It supports the point that completion does not guarantee understanding, not a causal claim that AI caused low capability.
Results under other conditions
Kazemitabaar et al., 2023 [R3] 		In a study of 69 novice learners, the AI group performed better on practice tasks. No significant difference was found in the main overall scores on subsequent unaided assessments, immediately or one week later. Learners also practiced making their own edits and received teacher support. A blanket claim that AI use always harms learning is inappropriate. A nonsignificant result does not establish equivalence either.

The starting point is not “use less AI.”
Keep building with AI while learning established approaches and what to examine through your own work.

Research supports a gap between completion and understanding. Whether this product improves juniors' design know-how over the long term requires separate validation.

03 / WHY PAIR PROGRAMMING

Learning through pair programming

Knowing an engineering method or design principle differs from knowing when and how to apply it in real work. Pair programming lets developers apply those principles to current code and requirements, examine overlooked conditions, and see the results of their choices. The aim is to gain know-how they can use again in the next task.

In traditional pair programming, two developers share a task and alternate between the Driver, who implements, and the Navigator, who guides and reviews. Rather than copying that arrangement exactly, Wellactually adapts the experience of making judgment explicit together and receiving feedback to roles suited to AI-assisted development.

  1. 01 · Context

    Start with your own problem

    The user already has a task to solve. Abstract knowledge connects to a concrete choice.

  2. 02 · Articulation

    Explain your reasoning

    Explaining your current thinking exposes assumptions and gaps in understanding.

  3. 03 · Feedback

    Compare perspectives

    Your partner's questions and counterexamples help you examine overlooked conditions and revise your judgment.

  4. 04 · Application

    Connect to execution

    Express your decision as a work instruction and interpret the observed result.

Product premise

Practice judgment in context

Experience reasoning that says “decide based on bottlenecks, freshness, and operational cost,” not “always add a cache.” Methods and design philosophies are lenses for making choices, not correct answers to memorize.

Research on human pairing

Educational evidence and limits

Educational research on human pair programming has observed benefits in course retention and assignment quality. However, one representative study found no significant difference in individual final-exam scores and did not randomly assign individuals. It does not directly establish the effects of pairing with AI. [R4]

We do not create a separate curriculum. A session does not try to teach all the foundational knowledge a user might need. It provides the minimum explanation needed to continue the current judgment and can set aside topics that need deeper study.

04 / WHY PAIR PROGRAMMING WITH AI

AI pairing and roles

AI surfaces overlooked assumptions and points to examine in the context of real work, helping the human apply engineering methods and design principles. The human understands the reasoning and chooses the direction, while the Driver handles implementation. This is a proposal to make participation in judgment and feedback an experience people can choose when needed.

Why separate the roles?

Separate implementation from judgment practice

Users can also discuss plans directly with a Driver. The difference is not the number of AIs: it is a workflow that may delegate even the thinking to get a task done versus one that leaves consequential judgment with the human.

We do not make the Driver deliberately ineffective to justify a Coach. The human practices setting the scope and conditions of delegation; the Driver implements within them.

Still to be validated

Intervention and learning effects

Even studies asking users to explain, predict, or make decisions about AI-generated code have sometimes found no significant improvement on an independent assessment. Whether a separate Coach and selective sessions lead to better judgment must be tested through actual use. [R5]

Conversational approaches shaped by the current decision, not separate modes
Pairing approach Judgment the Coach surfaces What the human practices
Problem framing · Core Are the problem, evidence, and goal clear before choosing a solution? Distinguish observation from speculation and decide what to improve.
Design reasoning · Core What are the rationale, alternatives, constraints, and operational costs? Understand trade-offs and choose a direction and conditions for revisiting it.
Delegation design · Core How much should be delegated at once, and how will it be checked? Write scope, constraints, and completion criteria into your own instruction.
Exploration and debugging · As needed Which observations can distinguish hypotheses about the cause? Request investigation, inspect the results, and revise the hypothesis.
Specification and counterexamples · As needed What is correct behavior for boundary inputs, concurrent execution, and failure sequences? Decide expected outcomes and reflect them in design and verification criteria.

Coach principles: Ask one important question at a time for the current decision. Do not repeat what the user already knows. Explain only as much as needed when they do not know. Proceed once the reasoning and risks are understood. Do not repeat the same objection without new evidence.

05 / USER EXPERIENCE

User experience

A session does not force the user through the entire development lifecycle. Choose only what to examine together this time, and continue through execution and result review if needed.

A typical sequence · Stages may be skipped, repeated, or ended as appropriate
Stage User action Service response / What remains
1. Choose to pair Start a conversation with the Coach and choose the task context and scope of material to share. Start coaching with the context the user shares. Do not require a Driver connection first or collect other conversations or material without permission.
2. Session Focus Describe the current uncertainty, such as “I want to add a cache, but I don't know what to consider.” Identify the judgment to examine, available evidence, and open questions. The user need not know all their gaps beforehand.
3. Deliberation Loop and delegation Examine evidence, assumptions, alternatives, and constraints with the Coach; decide scope and completion criteria. When delegating, connect the Driver conversation and personally write and send the instruction. Iterate through questions, answers, and revised conditions. Sending the Driver an instruction ends one turn of the Deliberation Loop, and the Driver works within the requested scope.
4. Joint Result Review Review the Driver Result with the Coach and discuss gaps against the goal, new conditions, and the next task. The human and Coach refer to results and evidence within the Shared Scope. Distinguish passing, failing, and unverified outcomes and carry the review into the next Deliberation Loop.
5. End pairing End the session because understanding is sufficient, or because of time, fatigue, or a decision to defer. Stop Coach observation and automatic intervention. Pairing can end before implementation is finished; Driver work continues independently.
6. Knowledge Compilation Review the session report and return to work on your project. Use available records to compile judgment changes, reusable perspectives, and open questions into HTML. The user chooses where to save it.
EXAMPLE / ONE REALISTIC TASK

Adding a product-list cache

“The product list is slow, so I'm thinking of adding a Redis cache.”

This is a fictional illustration. A p95 of 800ms, a one-minute TTL, and a single instance are example assumptions, not measurements or recommended settings. The dialogue depicts the user's own thinking and writing, not an automatic instruction-generation feature.

AClarify the problem and constraintsProblem framing + design reasoning

Human → Coach

Processing descriptions takes most of the time. I want to bring the list's p95 below 800ms.

Coach

How stale can a cached value be? Can you apply the same standard to descriptions and prices?

Human

Descriptions can be up to a minute old, but prices and stock can't. I shouldn't cache the whole response.

Coach

Then let's separate what would be cached first. Given your current operating conditions, are there alternatives to Redis worth comparing?

Judgment that stays with the human

Goal
Improve response time, rather than introduce Redis.
Condition discovered
Change frequency and acceptable staleness are different. Freshness requirements must be distinguished by data type.
Direction
With a single instance, compare an in-process cache and processing optimizations too. The Coach does not make the final choice.

If there is no evidence of the bottleneck, do not invent answers for this conversation. The human first chooses instrumentation or investigation work and delegates it to the Driver.

BInvestigate and revise the designDelegation + exploration + design review

Human → Driver · Human-authored instruction

Investigate the call structure for description processing and price and stock lookup. Don't change any code. Report whether descriptions can be separated, the impact on callers, and the information needed to choose the cache size.

Driver → Human

Description processing can be separated. However, the result differs by the requested language.

Coach → Human

If the same product is requested in different languages, can the cache key you had in mind distinguish the results?

Human

Product ID alone won't work. I need to include the language and check that results from different languages don't get mixed up.

Judgment that stays with the human

Delegation scope
Separate out investigation to reduce uncertainty before delegating the entire implementation.
Design change
A key must distinguish inputs that change the result, not merely name the object being stored.
Execution boundary
Do not automatically expand investigation into implementation. If implementation is needed, the human writes a new instruction.

Answers expressed in this conversation alone do not establish lasting understanding. Learning effects should be evaluated by whether the user identifies the conditions independently in a later, similar task.

CInstruct the Driver and end the sessionWrite an instruction from understood conditions → end the session

Human → Driver · Human-authored follow-up instruction

Cache only the processed descriptions in-process. Include the product ID and language in the key, and use a one-minute TTL. Keep price and stock lookup and the public response format unchanged. Check with me before implementing the size limit and eviction policy, which we haven't decided yet.

Human → Coach

This time I wanted to work through how to decide what to cache and how to choose the key. I understand that part now, so I'll stop pairing here. I'll check the implementation results later.

Service · Post-session Knowledge Compilation

Compile the judgments discussed in this session and the checks still outstanding. Mark implementation and performance verification as not yet complete.

Distinguish two kinds of completion

Pairing ends
The human understands the judgment examined together and chooses to end.
Development task status
Implementation, tests, and performance measurement under the same scenario may remain. Ending the session does not mark the task complete.
Another path
If the user wants to keep pairing, review the results together. Distinguish passing tests from achieving the goal.

07 / USAGE RHYTHM & EXIT

Usage cadence and exit

Pairing has a cost: explaining your thinking and coordinating with a partner. It is time chosen for judgment practice, even at the expense of some task speed—not something designed for every day or every task. A prescribed usage frequency or streak is not a success criterion.

Exit A · Sufficient understanding

Ready to decide

A session can end when the user understands the reasoning and constraints of this decision or has separated the unknowns into investigation tasks. Neither project completion nor the Coach's permission is required.

Exit B · Stop for now

Pause or defer

Users can stop even without an answer. Record what remains open; do not make users pretend to understand or pass a quiz before they can leave.

Long-term exit

Independent problem solving

As know-how accumulates, users may need pairing less for similar tasks. Reduced usage alone does not establish growth. We must distinguish greater capability from disengagement caused by fatigue or dissatisfaction.

Exit behavior: Stop the observation connection and automatic Coach intervention, and compile the records available up to that point. Show whether the Driver is still running. The human separately handles Driver cancellation, approval, and follow-up requests in the existing UI.

08 / KNOWLEDGE COMPILATION

Knowledge Compilation

After a session ends, extract context, judgment changes, and reusable know-how from accessible Driver records and the human–Coach conversation into a single HTML report. This is a per-session artifact, separate from this product-description HTML.

What the report does

Connect concrete events in the current project to reusable perspectives. Preserve initial thinking, reasons for change, evidence checked, conditions of application, and open questions. Generation failure does not block exit: show failures and missing data, and allow a retry or later compilation.

What the report cannot establish

Reading or keeping a report does not guarantee long-term learning. Research exists on reflection during coding, but it does not establish that automatic HTML summaries transfer into independent capability. The report is a tool for reflection and reuse. [R6]

09 / REASONABLE QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Distinguish critiques that challenge the product's purpose from issues to tune during actual use. Rather than dismiss objections, explain our choices and what still needs to be tested.

Why not just discuss the plan with the Driver?

You can. Asking the same questions does not automatically produce the same learning experience. In ordinary use, people may delegate even the thinking to get a result. Wellactually deliberately structures a pairing experience that keeps consequential judgment in the human's role.

The differentiator is not having two AIs. It is whether the user makes better judgments and gives better instructions without the Coach in a later, similar task. The added benefit of a separate Coach remains a product hypothesis.

Don't I need to know what I don't know before seeking a Coach?

You do not need to diagnose the gap precisely. “I'm not sure this design is right” or “I don't know what to consider” is enough reason to start. Discovering the judgment to examine together is part of the session. The product does not assume a service that monitors all work and automatically summons the user.

Won't I become a relay carrying the Coach's answers to the Driver?

That would defeat the purpose. The Coach does not provide a finished instruction and merely ask you to send it. The human understands the reasoning and conditions, then instructs the Driver in their own words. Meanwhile, code, logs, and existing decisions can be connected as shared context to preserve judgment while reducing material-transfer work.

Does the Coach have to teach everything?

No. Its main role is to surface overlooked assumptions and points worth considering. It offers the minimum explanation and examples needed for the current decision, rather than lecturing on all the foundational theory in one session. Topics for deeper study can be recorded separately in the report.

Won't I just become dependent on the Coach instead of the coding agent?

Usage frequency alone cannot rule out that possibility. However, use is selective; the product does not hold users in pairing for familiar tasks. Rather than imposing a mandatory graduation exam, we look at whether they identify the conditions independently in the next similar task. We distinguish growth from disengagement caused by fatigue when usage falls.

What if the Coach is wrong?

Human pairing does not guarantee error-free guidance either. Users must be able to ask questions until satisfied, check the evidence, and reject suggestions. The product does not promise to create that mindset on their behalf.

At the same time, the Coach is responsible for disclosing evidence and uncertainty, revising assumptions when challenged, and correcting wrong conclusions. Keeping final judgment with the human does not remove this responsibility.

Won't pairing slow down work and lead to endless questions?

Explaining your thinking can take time. That is why pairing is not imposed on every task. Distinguish thinking that supports learning from unnecessary conversation, and tune the persona and instructions to avoid repeating established points or objections without new evidence.

Proceed once the human has chosen a direction, separated out investigation, or understood the risk and deferred the decision. The criterion is neither speed at any cost nor many questions, but a judgment experience worth the time invested.

Does ending a session stop implementation? Does reading the report mean I learned?

No to both. Ending pairing stops Coach observation and automatic intervention; it neither cancels nor completes Driver work. The report compiles records available up to that point. Its existence does not prove learning. Record what was covered, the understanding the user expressed, and facts actually verified separately.

11 / SOFTWARE ARCHITECTURE · BRIEF

Software architecture

File-based MVP · Read-only profile not yet verified Keep the existing Driver environment. The Coach uses the SDK's built-in read/search tools for current code and selected local session records. For the hackathon, add the necessary file-change triggers and Knowledge Compilation rather than building AHP integration or a separate Workspace Reader first. [T4]

  1. 1 · Keep the Driver in its existing environment

    Keep the existing Driver input, tool, and approval UI rather than building a separate Driver runtime. Separate role instructions and check the actual permission mode of the chosen execution path. [T2]

  2. 2 · Use selected local records

    Begin deliberation with the Coach, then confirm the selected session's log path when delegating work to the Driver. Use relevant requests, replies, and tool results as context, and coalesce file changes to propose joint review. Do not send the entire original record to the model every time.

  3. 3 · Built-in SDK read/search

    The Coach has a separate SDK session and UI. Inspect current code as needed through built-in SDK read/search tools, without creating a separate Workspace Reader framework. Automatic intervention occurs only during pairing, with bounded call volume. [T4] [T5]

  4. 4 · Authority boundaries stronger than roles

    Verify a configuration that restricts read tools and shared code and log paths while excluding edit, shell, and Driver-control tools. Setting a working directory alone does not make a session read-only. Check that sensitive information and unshared content in logs are not passed to the model.

  5. 5 · Compile knowledge after exit

    When the human ends pairing, stop file observation and automatic coaching, and freeze the evidence used, conversation, and decisions. The Compiler creates report data only from that input, without exploring current code afresh. The extension renders HTML and saves it to the user's chosen path.

  6. 6 · Make failures and storage explicit

    Do not disguise missing logs, read failures, or generation failures as completion. Retain only necessary evidence and references, not a wholesale copy of raw logs in another database. Distinguish consent to share, retention, deletion, and model costs.

What has been verified so far: In one Copilot CLI/SDK session, we read local records and detected a new tool result written to the file without AHP or database queries. The new Coach's read-only permissions, other harnesses, large outputs, and recovery after restart still need verification. AHP is a later alternative if the file-based approach cannot meet requirements or support needs to expand.

The initial scope is text-based. Voice, intercepting unsent drafts, and having the Coach write tests are not required features. Do not assume native login is automatically shared with SDK authentication or that separate model calls are free.

Implementation details and technical verification are covered in the separate technical design document.

12 / REFERENCES & FURTHER READING

References

Citations distinguish research findings from product-design hypotheses. This is not a systematic review of all relevant research. The existence of technical documentation does not mean integration has been completed in a real environment. Reviewed as of September 12, 2026.

Research · Original papers and author materials

  1. R1Shen & Tamkin — How AI Impacts Skill Formation (2026)

    Randomized-experiment preprint. Use of a new Python library followed by an immediate unaided comprehension assessment. Do not generalize it into evidence of long-term design capability.

  2. R2Prather et al. — The Widening Gap: The Benefits and Harms of Generative AI for Novice Programmers (2024)

    Observation of 21 novices' working process. Program completion coexisted with comprehension and metacognitive difficulties. Not a causal experiment.

  3. R3Kazemitabaar et al. — Studying the effect of AI Code Generators on Supporting Novice Learners in Introductory Programming (CHI 2023)

    Sixty-nine novice learners. In an environment with hands-on editing practice and teacher support, no significant difference was found on the main independent assessments after AI use.

  4. R4McDowell et al. — Pair Programming Improves Student Retention, Confidence, and Program Quality (CACM 2006)

    Educational context for human pair programming. Observed course retention, assignment quality, and subsequent course enrollment, among other outcomes. Retention primarily means remaining in a course or major, not memory retention.

  5. R5Kazemitabaar et al. — Exploring the Design Space of Cognitive Engagement Techniques with AI-Generated Code for Enhanced Learning (2024)

    Intervention study requiring explanations, predictions, and action decisions, among other activities. The reviewed preprint did not establish significant improvement on an independent coding assessment.

  6. R6Xie et al. — Developing Novice Programmers’ Self-Regulation Skills with Code Replays (ICER 2023, PDF)

    Exploratory research on reflection through replaying one's own coding process. It does not validate long-term learning effects of automatic summary reports.
